A capable agent is not automatically entitled to act. The right authority depends on what can go wrong, whether errors reach consequential decisions, whether anyone can catch them, and what happens afterward. Treat authority as a permission that can be granted, held, reduced or suspended as evidence changes—not as a one-way launch milestone. [cite:mMw3arwQsL0:c05] [cite:3ZlQk4-knWM:c06]

## Grant, hold and withdraw authority against risk evidence

Start at the lowest authority level that creates value. A practical ladder is suggestion, editable draft, actions approved individually, and actions within explicitly preapproved limits. Before moving up, define the permitted actions, accountable decision-maker, evidence needed, review expectations and fallback. Legal or organizational constraints may cap authority regardless of model capability; for example, a hypothetical claims workflow requires human review of denials. That example is an assumed rule, not a statement of universal law. [cite:3ZlQk4-knWM:c06] [cite:T0HhO4YtTfE:c02]

One proposed reporting workflow begins in **shadow mode**: the human completes the report as usual while the agent independently produces its version. Compare the two, investigate disagreements and build an expert-reviewed record. The next stage lets the agent draft while an analyst checks every line. If evidence warrants it, review can later focus on flagged items plus independently selected samples, and eventually on high-risk exceptions with periodic independent sampling. The stages are a proposed operating pattern, not validated promotion thresholds. [cite:-a2oTS9J67U:c10]

Use a joint scorecard. Value measures such as cycle time, hours returned and detection latency belong beside severity of errors, overrides, recovery performance and missed issues. When review becomes exception-only, sample **unflagged** work independently: otherwise the most consequential omissions may never reach a reviewer. A declining override rate may mean better agent performance or merely shallower review. A named authority owner should promote only when the evidence and controls meet the workflow's agreed conditions; hold if evidence is insufficient, and restrict or suspend authority when recurring violations or control failures justify it. The sources do not establish universal thresholds or sampling rates. [cite:-a2oTS9J67U:c11] [cite:h8jgniNJcbQ:c11]

Clinical practice illustrates why the ladder is not simply “test, then automate.” Real calls and synthetic rare cases can exercise failures before exposure; simulation failures send the team back to improve the system. Passing simulation can support supervised real-world evaluation, but simulated patients are not patients and a clean simulation does not establish clinical benefit. Clinician-supervised testing and monitored deployment supply different evidence. In an irreversible-harm setting, monitoring and rollback cannot undo a harmful call already made, so a small initial exposure alone is not a substitute for predeployment evidence. This is the speaker's clinical release rationale, not a blanket legal rule for every study. [cite:McknwOzbmyg:c01] [cite:McknwOzbmyg:c09] [cite:McknwOzbmyg:c10]

Authority level is distinct from selecting which workflow nodes are sufficiently checkable for automation and from limiting the population exposed to a release. See [[agent-delegation-and-coordination]] for node suitability and [[production-governance-and-release]] for exposure and recovery. An internal changelog agent that only raises reviewable pull requests, for example, is not evidence that autonomous production publication is safe. [cite:TeErpYBUIeM:c10]

## Assess the whole path from error to harm

Ask four separate questions: how might the system fail; can that failure reach a real decision; will a control or person catch it; and how severe would the consequence be? This is a conceptual decomposition, not a calibrated probability formula. The same model can carry different risk when its authority, human mediation, monitoring or exposure changes. Controls can contain defects, but that does not excuse poor model quality or make a nominal human-in-the-loop label proof of effective review. [cite:mMw3arwQsL0:c01] [cite:mMw3arwQsL0:c04] [cite:mMw3arwQsL0:c05]

One retrospective analysis reported that its thirteen dimensions covered almost 98% of examined incidents and that an incident implicated six dimensions on average. Its sampling, coding and denominators were not fully specified. These are reported categorizations of past incidents—not proof the framework predicts future incidents or that any control caused a reduction in harm. Their useful lesson is to investigate interacting technical and organizational failures rather than stop at the first model error. [cite:mMw3arwQsL0:c02]

## Prioritize by severity, likelihood and materiality

Name concrete harms before choosing tests. A clinical assistant might miss sudden vision loss, invent medical advice or ignore distress. Distinguish an incorrect answer that causes no harm from one that could cause serious harm. The same scheduling error may waste time in one case and affect a vulnerable patient or procedure in another; those examples do not quantify harm probabilities. [cite:McknwOzbmyg:c02] [cite:AN65uc645mE:c12] [cite:qStB9GbppMU:c02]

Allocate scarce testing and review using severity, plausible frequency, exposure and business context, not failure count alone. Severity-times-frequency can help order discussion, but is only a heuristic, not a validated risk model; a rare catastrophic failure must not disappear merely because its observed count is low. High aggregate accuracy can still leave substantial absolute harm at high volume, while a small clean test cannot establish a rare-error rate. Representative sustained testing and expert assessment are needed for stronger evidence. [cite:qZj7sqmidqA:c03] [cite:AN65uc645mE:c10] [cite:AN65uc645mE:c11]

In security triage, even real findings can overwhelm engineers. Deduplicate, consider impact and likelihood in the actual environment, agree severity rules with product and security teams, and route findings to their owners. A firewall or internal-only access can change assessed likelihood without making a vulnerability unreal. The proposed materiality scales do not provide universal weights or thresholds. [cite:imFedndyXYQ:c06] [cite:imFedndyXYQ:c08] [cite:mMw3arwQsL0:c11]

## Count a sound handoff as success

If authorization is absent, uncertainty is too consequential, or an action is irreversible or outside the agent's remit, stopping and handing off can be the correct outcome. In an expense-workflow demonstration, an expired session led the agent to hand authentication to the user rather than guess a password; it resumed after control returned and the expense state was checked. The user was simulated, so this demonstrates a possible behavior, not a calibrated production reliability rate. Do not optimize merely for fewer escalations: that would reward inappropriate autonomy. [cite:Cc0_nyxROBA:c08]

A reviewer must be able to make a real decision. In a hypothetical exception workflow, the agent highlights source evidence and uncertainty and supplies an editable draft instead of filing automatically. Whether a user can recognize and correct a mistake depends on expertise, access and time; an approval button alone is not a safety guarantee or a compliance determination. For approval design see [[human-review-and-agent-governance]]; for independent runtime enforcement see [[agent-authority-isolation-and-runtime-controls]]. [cite:3ZlQk4-knWM:c07] [cite:jHMiYtjoJfA:c03]

## Exercise detection, fallback and ownership

For each important failure, name the detection path, fallback and responsible responder, then test whether they operate together. In the hypothetical exception assistant, an analyst can reject an invented exception against the source; unreadable extraction stops the workflow and requests a clean file; an alert has an owner empowered to stop work. A targeted offline test can reveal that the assistant misses exceptions, but does not identify every live omission. In that example, affected work stays under manual review; other workflows need their own detection and fallback design. [cite:3ZlQk4-knWM:c08] [cite:3ZlQk4-knWM:c09]

## Tie evidence to each hazard

A useful safety case links each named hazard to the relevant calls or cases, datasets, pinned prompt, judge verdicts and control-response evidence, and identifies the release and authority scope those artifacts support. When a prompt, language, data source or action scope changes, follow the links to determine which argument needs renewed evidence. The clinical account states this traceability requirement; it does not provide an audited submission or establish regulatory acceptance. [cite:McknwOzbmyg:c11]

## Keep risk expertise in the team

Using a hosted model instead of training one does not remove the need for statistical testing, model-risk literacy or validation of evaluators. Combine that expertise with domain, product, security and systems knowledge: functional agent behavior is not captured by classifier metrics alone, even though precision and recall can be useful when validating a judge against labeled cases. This is organizational guidance, not a measured staffing formula. See [[validate-judges-against-human-labels]] for the validation method.
