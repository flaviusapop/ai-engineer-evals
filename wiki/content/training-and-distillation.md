Training changes what a model tends to do; it does not automatically establish that the changed behavior is useful. Begin with a specific failure and development/validation cases, then choose the smallest intervention that addresses it. Use those cases for iterative intervention selection and harness retesting; reserve a separate untouched test set for final confirmation, as discussed in [[tests-oracles-and-verifier-validity]]. Prompt and tool-loop changes give fast feedback and may suffice. If they reach a practical ceiling, consider fine-tuning and then retest the harness around the changed model on development/validation cases. This is a heuristic, not a required sequence or a guarantee that training will help. [cite:CvRngaQZQ3Y:c05] [cite:Ti-BmGU1_mQ:c09] [cite:wcUJWP6WpGM:c13]

## Test the reward, not just the score

A reward is a learning signal, not the goal itself. In reinforcement learning (RL), sampled actions receive feedback and updates make relatively rewarded behavior more likely. A shaped reward might award credit for accessing a table, using complete queries and other intermediate steps; a simpler baseline might assess only the final answer. In the cited financial-agent study, that binary signal was a reference-based model-judged answer score, not an independently established correctness oracle; judge calibration and error rates were unspecified. The speaker reports more improvement from this binary signal than from a richer expert-weighted intermediate rubric. That is a local ablation, not proof that binary rewards are generally better: the exact rubric, weighting, run variance and judge error analysis were not supplied. Compare rewards under comparable training conditions, and audit the resulting behavior on independent task criteria. [cite:TyPpSRXGhbc:c11] [cite:TyPpSRXGhbc:c06]

Distinguish learning an action skill from acquiring missing facts. Rewarding tool use may improve behavior, but does not itself supply dense domain knowledge. One proposed complement is supervised learning from environmental tokens alongside RL; that account is qualitative, not a measured comparison or complete recipe. For components whose outcomes are mechanically checkable, verifiable feedback is a candidate; subjective or context-sensitive components may still need expert judgment. [cite:TNwJ1LMiENk:c02] [cite:AQv3qRCG6Gw:c12] [cite:lCBf9slCanI:c05]

### Reward concise correct reasoning relatively

Reasoning length is a separate objective from reward validity. Rather than impose the same token target on every problem, sample several solutions to the *same* task, identify correct ones, and give a relative brevity bonus among those. Keep an explicit correctness reward: an incorrect short answer should not win just because it is short. The useful length can change with difficulty and model ability. This is a proposed design, not a demonstrated quality–efficiency frontier, and balancing its objectives still requires testing. [cite:V-EDrhIhHzQ:c05]

## Pick artifacts that match the learning objective

For supervised distillation, curate successful teacher interactions and train a student to imitate them; indiscriminately copying failed traces teaches the wrong behavior. For online RL, the data product is instead a task prompt plus an executable environment and a reward: the student generates its own tool trajectory. A demonstrated search-data workflow built solved trajectories for supervised tuning; its RL extension was proposed, not a completed training result. Keep the untouched final test separate from both construction and tuning. [cite:CvRngaQZQ3Y:c06] [cite:qFaKvbVc5cE:c07]

Teacher-generated examples can seed a smaller domain model; compare candidate sizes on development/validation cases kept separate from training, escalate cases the small model cannot handle, and consider refreshing examples with shadow teacher traffic. Verify rights and provider terms before using teacher outputs for training. The speaker's routing example does not supply a complete policy or validated savings. [cite:Ti-BmGU1_mQ:c08]

For token-level methods, preserve the actual generated token history as well as logical messages. Retokenizing rendered text or applying a chat template can change whitespace or tokens and produce a different training history. An endpoint sufficient for evaluation may not expose log probabilities, temperature control or hidden reasoning tokens required by a particular training stack; API compatibility alone is not enough. These requirements are method-specific, not a rule for all black-box optimization. [cite:V-EDrhIhHzQ:c08] [cite:V-EDrhIhHzQ:c09]

## Choose where the teacher intervenes

Trace freshness and hint adaptivity are independent choices. Historical traces plus a fixed instruction can target a known problem without a replayable production environment. Current-policy rollouts reveal what the student does now; a hint can be tailored to that particular mistake. Between them, one can start from a historical prefix and generate a fresh student step, but this is not a fully on-policy trajectory. A proposed offline refund-policy correction is an illustration, not a measured deployment result. [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c01] [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c02] [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c06]

A long trace's final grade may not say which decision needs correction. Select a local intervention point, add a teacher hint there, and supervise the next step or a short continuation. Selectively masking unrelated teacher tokens can avoid forcing the student to copy incidental wording. For example, a tool-availability reminder can guide the decision to use a tool rather than rewriting the whole trajectory. These are reported mechanisms; selection and mask quality require validation. [cite:q4Tr-DknG2M:c08] [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c08] [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c09]

The speaker reports that a completion-focused intervention increased submission-tool use from roughly 22% to 60%, with test-pass rate approximately stable. In another customer-format example, rollout-specific hints reportedly raised formatting correctness from about 15% to 80%; earlier reward or supervised approaches had hurt coding performance, and final coding regression was not numerically reported. These results lack sample sizes and uncertainty. Measure the target behavior *and* retained core capability separately. [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c05] [cite:ZTA0GwpAUak:c07]

In on-policy distillation, the student generates a rollout and a teacher provides likelihoods for those student-generated tokens as a learning signal; if a hint changes teacher context, align and slice token probabilities correctly. A different proposal trains separate RL experts from the same base for different environments, then distills them into one checkpoint. Neither an expert ensemble nor a teacher's high score guarantees that the student retains each skill: evaluate the resulting checkpoint on each environment. The specialist-consolidation recommendation has no controlled comparison in the source. [cite:V-EDrhIhHzQ:c15] [cite:V-EDrhIhHzQ:c03]

## Make learning opportunities reachable

An RL environment must expose actions and feedback relevant to the skill: a kernel-optimization task, for example, needs a way to edit a kernel and observe performance. Long tasks also require attention to trajectory duration and scarce hardware. With sparse rewards, an initially unreachable good response cannot be reinforced in the sampled-answer setup; priming, supervised examples or better initialization may make useful behavior reachable. Seek a task distribution where current rollouts include both successes and failures rather than all solving or none solving; no universal pass-rate threshold is provided. Run small training experiments and inspect tool-call changes to uncover environment or reward defects before scaling. [cite:AVMr9PMINyo:c01] [cite:AVMr9PMINyo:c07] [cite:uIiA6DquRiE:c14] [cite:AQv3qRCG6Gw:c09] [cite:AQv3qRCG6Gw:c11]

Task selection and reward choice are distinct experiments. At comparable resources, vary data quality and curriculum, then compare transfer and retained abilities on development/validation cases. Confirm the selected system on the separate untouched final test. A finance study compared single-table, mixed and progressively harder training; the simplest distribution produced the largest reported improvement, possibly because basic tool-use discipline was the bottleneck. That does not establish that simpler curricula always win. [cite:TyPpSRXGhbc:c10]

In the same reported setting, a trained four-billion-parameter specialist achieved almost 60% pass-at-one against 51% for a 235-billion-parameter variant on 290 expert-curated held-out examples. The speaker also reported transfer to a separate 80-example multi-table test. These are bounded, speaker-reported results with no uncertainty or full baseline configuration, not evidence of general small-model superiority. [cite:TyPpSRXGhbc:c06] [cite:TyPpSRXGhbc:c08]

## Ground domain knowledge and adapt to the interface

Relevant source material can give a teacher an informational advantage when clean labels are unavailable, generating candidate supervision for a learner without that context. Check generated labels against the intended task and separate validation sources; useful context does not guarantee reliable rewards. [cite:AQv3qRCG6Gw:c04]

Spotify describes a staged catalog approach: first freeze the pretrained backbone and learn embeddings linking new semantic IDs to text; then unfreeze for multitask instruction tuning, including retrieval and recommendations. The speakers report degraded results when the grounding stage was removed or merged and the largest drop with random rather than pretrained initialization. Multitask benefits, including for newer audiobook recommendations, are reported without effect sizes; only two backbone families were checked. Test domain gains *and* retained general capability, not just the new task score. [cite:2LRIAfng7eA:c03] [cite:2LRIAfng7eA:c04] [cite:2LRIAfng7eA:c05]

The feedback interface matters in visual work. One vendor describes jointly training image and text to support a build–inspect–improve website loop, without a controlled joint-training ablation. An OCR account stages language pretraining, image-text training, supervised OCR and RL; its machine-verifiable group rewards are only as useful as the tests' coverage of user needs, and the reported boost lacks a quantitative ablation. [cite:AVMr9PMINyo:c06] [cite:T72nqdC92PM:c04] [cite:T72nqdC92PM:c06]

For robot recovery, a learned progress signal can flag dips during trials so operators collect demonstrations of recovery and fine-tune again. Check detection misses and false positives, and test on the real site and interface. The reported progress detector has no sensitivity numbers; the claimed advantage of broad pretraining followed by specialization is not a controlled comparison. [cite:Sjfz1TqxzEs:c04] [cite:Sjfz1TqxzEs:c07]

## Improve the feedback mechanism itself

A verifier can filter apparently successful trajectories, but execution alone does not establish task correctness. Compare verifiers by training on equal-sized sets each selects and testing the resulting models on development/validation cases kept separate from training. In one study the process-verifier-selected sets produced better supervised models at both 3,000 and 9,000 examples than a weaker verifier's selections. Automated verifier search can explore candidates, but expert insights and independent labels remain useful; the reported one-day unseeded search reached about 70% of the manually developed verifier's agreement in that particular setting. [cite:mMNkdYnIVC4:c08] [cite:xLxhT2ZI7UM:c09] [cite:xLxhT2ZI7UM:c10]

Conditional credit answers another question. **Illustrative:** if a financial agent selects the wrong table but calculates correctly from the table it selected, assess both end-to-end correctness and whether the subsequent calculation follows from that assumption. A proposed dynamic rubric can expose the latter skill; it must not relabel the overall answer correct. The proposal has not established a validated training benefit. [cite:2aS7aKoXn64:c10]
