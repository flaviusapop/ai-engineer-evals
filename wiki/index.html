The Evaluation Field Guide

A concept-first guide to designing, interpreting and operating evaluations of AI systems, agents and real workflows.

Search titles, summaries and article text.

    Guided reading paths

    • Start here: decide what evidence you need

      Frame the decision and success criteria, identify what the evidence can establish, choose representative cases and measures, verify outcomes, compare results, then assess value and risk. This is the shared core; use the agent, data, judge or verification task paths for specialist methods.

    • Frame an evaluation and learn from failures

      Use when a team has a problem but no defensible evaluation: frame the decision, bound conclusions, inspect failure evidence, then grow coverage. For an agent comparison continue with comparing-agents-without-confounding-the-result; use the section index for other foundations.

    • Design and compare agent evaluations

      Use for a reproducible agent comparison: select the benchmark, design its population and tasks, specify success and resource measures, control model–harness differences, inspect outcomes and protect integrity. For statistical estimands and confidence, also read compare-systems-confidence-and-behavior-without-mixing-estimands; it is distinct from attribution in comparing-agents-without-confounding-the-result.

    • Build an evaluation set for a decision

      Use when building an offline set: first select the population and slices, specify valid tasks, collect production-grounded cases and human judgments, then protect holdouts. Optional: generate-synthetic-cases-without-mistaking-them-for-reality for coverage gaps; generated cases are not a substitute for reality.

    • Build and validate a judge

      Use when designing a judge: define the target and rubric, consider proxy limits, provide evidence, select a method, collect human references, validate against held-out labels, then operate and improve. Revisit agreement-truth-and-the-limits-of-evaluator-proxies when interpreting validation: agreement is not truth. Optional compare-systems-confidence-and-behavior-without-mixing-estimands clarifies comparative and calibration questions rather than judge implementation.

    • Decide what evidence supports accepting agent work

      Use to set an acceptance gate for agent-produced work: define claims and evidence, exercise product behavior, inspect checks and their oracles, and set feedback and integration gates. For additional guarantees or review decisions, branch to formal-verification-and-executable-constraints or code-review-and-organizational-risk respectively.

    • Evaluate a domain-specific workflow

      Use when selecting evidence with domain experts: start with domain success and accountability, then choose the article for your workflow rather than reading all branches. The remaining articles are alternatives for financial work, grounded answers, voice, clinical review, legal documents, synthetic respondents, visual or physical systems, creative work, or recommendations.

    • Set risk-based agent controls and release authority

      Use before granting or expanding agent autonomy: prioritize harms and exposure, model threats, select independently enforced controls, establish human oversight, probe behavior, then decide release. Optional privacy-tenancy-and-data-governance covers sensitive data; specific attack and payment workflows remain in the safety section index.

    • Turn production failures into a release decision

      Use when a deployed agent is failing or changing: define useful outcomes, monitor live quality, diagnose the failure, turn evidence into regression tests, decide release, and continue monitoring. Optional build-and-maintain-production-grounded-evaluation-sets explains how to maintain cases; runtime architecture and operations can be browsed in the production section.

    • Choose and release a cost-effective model route

      Use for a task-level model-selection or routing decision: compare useful work, cost and quality; select model/runtime candidates and routing; run controlled regression evidence; decide promotion. Optional inference-serving addresses serving capacity or latency, and training-and-distillation addresses weight changes; neither is required for routine routing.

    • Improve a failing system without gaming the score

      Use after observing a failure: diagnose it, design controlled improvement evidence, protect the objective and holdout, choose an intervention, verify regressions, then decide promotion. Prompt/harness, retrieval and training articles are alternatives chosen for the diagnosed bottleneck, not consecutive required steps.

    Foundations

    Frame the question, define the system and bound the conclusions evidence supports.

    • What evaluation can establish: design, evidence and uncertainty

      Teaches evaluation structure, evidence scope, uncertainty in claims and evaluation maturity; specific experimental estimators and product economics belong elsewhere. Owns control, sampling, uncertainty and causal interpretation; does not teach item-response models.

    • From business problem to meaningful success criteria

      Owns problem framing, construct selection and customer interpretation; detailed experiment design and model comparisons are separate.

    • Benchmarks, leaderboards and model selection

      Owns benchmark transfer, rank interpretation, lifecycle and local selection; does not replace measurement of deployed-system reliability.

    • Tracing, failure analysis and evaluator methods

      Owns trace evidence, diagnostic scope and evaluator methods; execution environment design and statistical comparison are separate.

    • Defining agent roles, boundaries and oversight

      Owns contracts, responsibilities and completion claims; business goal setting and runtime implementation are adjacent but separate. Owns architecture vocabulary and system-level attribution; retrieval internals and model selection are treated elsewhere.

    • Building and maintaining evaluation coverage

      Owns evaluation settings, regression promotion, changing architecture and ownership; evaluator machinery is covered in the trace article.

    • Improvement loops, adaptation and overfitting

      Owns optimization loops and adaptation tradeoffs; empirical comparison design and memory architecture are separate.

    • Understanding context, memory and retrieval

      Owns knowledge and context access, memory and retrieval mechanisms; screen observations and general model architecture are separate.

    • Choosing tasks with verifiable outcomes

      Owns task verifiability and training suitability, not claims of measured ROI or model superiority.

    • Evaluating visual and multimodal agent capabilities

      Owns modality and platform distinctions; generic agent traces and retrieval are covered elsewhere.

    • Testing specialized representations and instruction robustness

      Owns distinct representations for language and biological data and anonymous-speaker labeling; does not claim cross-domain equivalence. Owns the lexical instruction-density experiment, its failure categories and relevant robustness probes; general agent failure diagnosis belongs with traces.

    • Item response models for evaluation

      Owns IRT estimation and diagnostics, not general case-by-case experiment diffs or causal claims about model ancestry.

    Agent evaluations

    Design tasks, comparisons, trajectories, simulated worlds and integrity controls.

    • Choosing and designing agent benchmarks

      Covers task selection and validity, not experimental execution.

    • Choosing agent success measures and resource budgets

      Defines distinct estimands and accounting; not experimental validity or task construction.

    • Comparing agents without confounding the result

      Covers treatment identity and execution integrity, not evaluation task meaning.

    • Verifying agent outcomes and diagnosing behavior

      Covers score surfaces and diagnostic traces, not verifier security or environment fidelity.

    • Building and validating simulated agent worlds

      Design scenarios and stateful environments, and validate user behavior and environmental fidelity against relevant real observations.

    • Protect benchmark integrity

      Covers leakage and scoring exploits; not general simulator validity.

    • Evaluating retrieval, memory and learning in agent workflows

      Covers informational capabilities; general experimental controls are taught separately.

    Tasks, datasets and evidence

    Build cases and references while separating coverage, sampling, labels, leakage and provenance.

    • Design evaluation distributions for decisions

      Coverage and estimands rather than individual-label accuracy.

    • Build and maintain production-grounded evaluation sets

      Collection, refresh and incident regression rather than synthetic generation.

    • Protect holdouts and historical evidence

      Separates adaptive test reuse, training exposure, entity leakage and future information.

    • Specify and audit meaningful benchmark tasks

      Task-level construct and verifier validity rather than population coverage.

    • Generate synthetic cases without mistaking them for reality

      Recipes, checks and sampling limitations; interactive simulators are treated separately.

    • Collect and interpret human judgments

      Human labeling and evidence quality rather than test-distribution design.

    • Evaluate and represent retrieval corpora

      Retrieval and corpus preparation rather than final-answer grading.

    • Build data for training and agent improvement

      Training-data selection rather than independent evaluation outcomes.

    • Preserve meaning and interpret measurements

      Measurement semantics and reproducibility rather than evaluator sampling.

    Judges and evaluators

    Define judgment targets, establish human references and maintain evaluators without treating agreement as truth.

    • Validate judges against human labels

      Alignment and classifier errors, not truth or probability calibration.

    • Agreement, truth and the limits of evaluator proxies

      Epistemic and construct-validity limits, not the operational labeling procedure.

    • Give judges the evidence and context they need

      Evidence access and verification, not judge error-rate estimation.

    • Turn task quality into actionable evaluation rubrics

      Criteria and score labels, not comparative estimands or evaluator architecture.

    • Compare systems, confidence and behavior without mixing estimands

      Statistical comparison and confidence, not judge implementation or truth claims.

    • Choose and combine judge methods

      Evaluator architectures and process grading, not their continuing operations.

    • Operate and improve evaluation systems

      Maintenance, triage and feedback loops, not initial architecture design.

    Verification and assurance

    • Verification contracts and evidence

      Owns intent, claims and evidence; execution mechanics belong in the feedback-loop and product-testing articles.

    • Building verification feedback and integration gates

      Owns orchestration, repository readiness and integration gates, not the detailed test oracle.

    • Testing product behavior and visual regressions

      Owns exercised workflows, visual behavior and durable effects; replay infrastructure belongs in the visual-regression article. Owns differential observation and replay controls, not general browser-based feature acceptance.

    • Tests, oracles and verifier validity

      Owns task construction, test-oracle validity and benchmark security; ordinary product execution is covered separately.

    • Formal verification and executable constraints

      Owns deductive guarantees and executable structural constraints, not the organization’s process for choosing review policies.

    • Code review, human judgment and organizational risk

      Owns review decisions, human accountability and organizational measurement; technical integration diagnosis belongs elsewhere.

    • Diagnosing failures across system boundaries

      Owns causal debugging and integration context; GPU-specific failure modes are treated in performance verification.

    • Verifying performance and kernel changes

      Owns benchmark integrity, numerical fidelity and concurrency/state failures in optimized workloads, rather than general verifier auditing.

    Production evaluation and operation

    Observe live workflows, learn from failures and manage changes and operational constraints.

    • Measuring useful outcomes and business value

      Business estimands and baselines, not release-control mechanics. Mandates, outcome measures and adoption rather than the mechanics of implementing controls.

    • Monitoring production quality and improvement

      Operational measurement and uncertainty, not human decision rights. Owns quality signals, test evolution, risk and task outcomes; execution traces and platform plumbing are covered elsewhere. Observability, discovery and continuous evaluation rather than offline release authorization.

    • Production governance, readiness and release

      Release and accountability decisions, not detailed security threat controls. Owns readiness, explicit promotion criteria, judge stability and recovery checks; live monitoring is elsewhere. Owns exposure, accountability and runtime safeguards; evaluation methods are separate.

    • Tracing and diagnosing production behavior

      Evidence capture and root-cause investigation, not a proof of user-visible success. Owns trace collection, privacy, triage, attribution and cost; it does not itself decide release readiness. Owns live discovery and incident handling; release gates and memory maintenance are separate.

    • Learning from production evidence and regression tests

      Improvement processes and feedback, not raw execution controls. Behavioral suites and predeployment evidence rather than continuous production monitoring. Owns task verification, comparisons and replay; incident management is separate.

    • Maintaining agent context, memory and knowledge

      Knowledge and retrieval state, not transactional execution guarantees. Owns retention, retrieval and maintenance of working knowledge; external-source provenance is separate. Procedural knowledge and recurring jobs, not generic trace storage. Owns reusable knowledge lifecycle; working-session context belongs to execution.

    • Operating secure, governed agent environments

      Security-specific threats and controls, distinct from general release decisions. Workload execution, access and infrastructure testing rather than semantic product quality.

    • Managing production retrieval, data and provenance

      Manage retrieval configuration, exact document and inventory access, and source provenance, including governed trace-data storage and document evidence pipelines. Trace instrumentation, interpretation and incident triage belong to the observability guide; retrieval optimization is covered separately.

    • Delivering agent-assisted work with human review

      Owns software-delivery practices and adoption; runtime controls and evaluation infrastructure are separate. Review capacity, decisions and handoffs rather than test-suite construction.

    • Voice-agent operations

      Owns audio timing, conversational concurrency and voice telemetry; generic tracing belongs elsewhere.

    • Evaluation infrastructure and team ownership

      Owns human responsibilities and reusable experimentation infrastructure, not specific monitoring choices. Owns team responsibilities and architectural capacity, not individual launch approvals.

    • Validating model and serving systems

      Owns model-serving compatibility, availability and research control rather than task-level agent quality. Model, backend and serving lifecycle rather than user-facing product adoption.

    • Managing agent task flow and action evidence

      Own task progression, action evidence and handoffs; not general-purpose evaluation metrics or permission policy.

    • Running and resuming agent sessions

      Own session lifecycle, persistence and event execution; distinguish working-session state from curated organizational memory.

    • Choosing agent delegation and coordination

      Own topology, specialist coordination and capacity tradeoffs; link model portfolio selection to routing-and-deployment.

    • Designing agent-facing product interactions

      Own interaction surfaces and application-facing workflows; not the tool catalog or authorization contract.

    Efficiency and performance

    Measure task and serving resource tradeoffs without losing quality or correctness.

    • Comparing task quality, cost and value

      Covers outcome economics and controlled comparisons, not inference-server tuning. Accounting and value proxies, not inference-server configuration.

    • Allocating models and deployment resources

      Covers task routing, provider selection and specialization rather than low-level serving internals. Application model quality and choices, rather than hardware-level serving metrics.

    • Designing efficient agent workflows

      Covers delivery loops and replay, not retrieval context or production serving. Trajectory and development workflow design, not evaluator scheduling.

    • Managing context, retrieval and cache costs

      Covers retrieval and context policies, not tool catalog interfaces or inference cache billing. Covers prefix placement, replica locality and billing rather than general KV sizing. Context, knowledge and retrieval economics, not model-selection economics.

    • Tool interfaces

      Tool affordances and invocation, schema discovery and progressive loading of instructions.

    • Measuring inference serving performance

      Covers inference phases, metrics, concurrency and KV capacity, not user-facing voice or specific decoding techniques. Service performance and resource provisioning, not business ROI or kernel algebra.

    • Speculative decoding

      Covers token-level speculation, not speculative voice pipeline execution.

    • Verifying hardware and training optimizations

      Covers kernels, precision and training-memory tradeoffs, not service-level SLO sweeps. Kernel mechanics and training architectures, not general model routing.

    • Interactive latency

      Covers voice stage readiness and overlap, not aggregate inference throughput.

    • Budgeting evaluation and monitoring

      Covers evaluation feedback and monitor budgets rather than product-task quality itself. Evaluation, verification and monitoring economics, not serving configuration.

    Improvement and optimization

    Use feedback to propose changes while protecting objectives, holdouts and promotion decisions.

    • Evaluation-driven improvement

      Covers experiment design and regression evidence, not the optimizer’s search algorithm.

    • Search and objective integrity

      Covers objectives, governance and acceptance decisions, not domain-specific candidate-generation methods.

    • Prompt and harness optimization

      Covers prompt, orchestration and memory updates, not weight-training algorithms.

    • Retrieval and query optimization

      Covers retrieval and tool-access mechanisms, not general context compaction or downstream model training.

    • Model selection and runtime efficiency

      Covers runtime selection and acceleration, not training model specialization.

    • Training and distillation

      Covers weight-update data, objectives and validation; prompt-only and runtime interventions belong elsewhere.

    • Using production feedback to improve systems

      Covers production feedback workflows and development leverage, not the generic acceptance loop.
